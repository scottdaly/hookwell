# HOOKWELL — Creative Constitution

*A chartered city of wizards, where magic is municipal plumbing.*

This document is the standard every object, system and screen in Hookwell is held to.
It is revised when real artifacts prove something better. It is not flavor text.
The reference images live in `shots/canon/`; a new object must sit in those pictures without
looking like a visitor.

---

## 1. The point of view

Hookwell is not a school, a tower, or a floating island. It is a **working town** whose
water main happens to be magic. A turquoise fluid called **ley** rises from a spring and is
carried through open gutters cut into the streets, exactly as a hill town carries water.
Ley powers ovens, lamps, scriptoria and stills. Where it is allowed to pool, it **blooms**:
cobalt fungus and waxy crust that eat the ground. The wizards of Hookwell are therefore
plumbers, clerks, chandlers and scriveners before they are anything grand. Everything they
do is **chartered**: sealed in red wax, entered in a ledger, argued about in the Chancery.

The fantasy is not "I am a powerful wizard". It is "**I am laying out a town for people who
happen to be wizards, and the town itself is slightly alive.**"

Three feelings the game must produce, in order:
1. *Humble competence*: small houses, careful gutters, bread and timber.
2. *Civic pride*: the first sealed charter, the first lit street, the Chancery going up.
3. *Managed strangeness*: the ley wants to pool and bloom; the city holds it in loops.

## 2. The three motifs

The whole visual language descends from three shapes. If a new object uses none of them,
it belongs to a different city.

- **THE HOOK (J).** Every roof apex ends in a brass hook with a wax bead on its tip. Lamps
  hang from crook-poles. Signs hang from hooks. Gutter spouts curl into copper hooks. Hats
  curl into a hook. The candle-pine's tip gutters over into a hook. Upper floors jetty out
  over the street like a hook's shoulder. *Proven:* the hooks are what make the skyline read
  as Hookwell at district scale; they were enlarged by a third once we saw the town from above.
- **THE ZIGZAG (WZ).** Chevron timber bands on every jettied storey. Z-braced shutters.
  Zigzag allotment fences. Staggered tile courses. *Proven:* the chevron band is the single
  most recognisable surface in the town; it appears on lodgings, journeyman houses, the tavern,
  the Chancery pavilion and the masters' hats (towers), never on work buildings.
- **THE PAIR AND THE SMALL BETWEEN (EeE).** Twin chimney flues in wax red with brass caps.
  Big-small-big window rhythm on every front. Two hook lamps flanking the Chancery door.
  Facades symmetrical with one deliberate small asymmetry: the door pushed to one side, a
  washing line, an odd shutter.

Secondary motifs: **four** (tented four-sided roofs, four-pane windows, the 4 m module),
**the loop** (ley must circulate; dead ends bloom), and **the seal** (a wax-red disc with a
glyph by every door; the interface is a ledger of seals).

## 3. World and scale

- One tile is 4 m. Almost everything is one tile. The scriptorium is two, the Chancery and
  the observatory are four. The city is dense and small; it must feel like a hill town.
- Ground is olive meadow drifting to dry tan, drawn with short ink ticks for grass. Every
  building stands on a **ragged packed-earth apron**; without it buildings float. Rock
  outcrops are grey-blue slabs. Wax-thistle clumps grow beside streets and yards.
- The map begins as an unremarkable meadow with one thing wrong with it: a spring that
  bubbles turquoise. A crook-post waymark with the town seal stands where the old road ends.
- Streets are 2.9 m of cobble inside a 4 m tile, with earth verges and chalk curbs. Wide
  full-tile paving read as highways and was rejected.

## 4. Architecture

- **Roofs are tented (pyramidal or hipped), never gabled.** Steep, with a flared lower
  course, glazed cobalt tile, a brass hook finial. Work buildings (woodcutter, stonecutter,
  still-house cap, porches, clerestories) use the **teal** glaze; homes and civic use cobalt.
- **Candle proportions.** Ordinary houses are tall for their footprint and taper. Apprentice
  lodgings are the thinnest and come in two roof pitches with the door on either side.
  Masters' towers are octagonal candles wearing a wider jettied hat.
- **Stone below, plaster above, timber on top.** Ashlar with chalk joints on the ground
  floor; lime-washed plaster (cream, ochre or rose) above; the jettied top floor carries the
  chevron band on a dark timber sill with brackets.
- **Purpose changes the type, not the paint.** Bakery: squat house plus a stone oven dome
  with a double flue and a bread shelf. Scriptorium: long low hall of E-e-E windows with a
  teal clerestory lantern. Still-house: a copper onion still rising through an open timber
  cage. Tavern: jettied front, teal porch on posts, an oversized hook lantern. Chancery: a
  hall with a chevron pavilion, the great seal, a red door, a pennant. Observatory: low wing
  and a drum with a cobalt dome split by a brass slit.
- **Every building has a mark.** A wax-red seal disc with its glyph on a chalk plaque beside
  the door, at hand height.
- **Windows** are four-pane, tall and narrow, cream frame, ink mullions, Z-braced ink-blue
  shutters on the important ones.

## 5. Materials and surface language

Surfaces are painted, not photographed. The reference is **ink and wash on paper**: a
depth/normal ink line around every form, washes of colour inside, chalk for joints, paper
grain over the whole frame. Materials read at gameplay scale (a house is ~120 px tall at
default zoom):

| Material | Colour | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Plaster | cream / ochre / rose | mottled lime wash, faint drips |
| Ashlar stone | grey-tan | irregular blocks, chalk joints, tool marks |
| Timber | dark walnut | flat with streaks; chevron bands on plaster |
| Roof tile | cobalt (homes, civic) / teal (work) | rounded staggered courses, rare pale chip |
| Brass | warm brass | hooks, finials, hoops, lamp cages |
| Copper | copper with verdigris | stills, spouts, cistern lids |
| Wax | seal red | seal discs, chimney flues, bead on every hook |
| Ley | cobalt body, turquoise highlight, pale foam | flows visibly away from the spring |
| Bloom | cobalt-violet caps, pale gills, waxy lilac crust | wrong and pretty; the only violet |
| Ink | blue-black | outlines, shutters, ink jars, inkwood crowns, crook-poles |

Colour rules:
- The dominant relationship is **warm plaster against cool cobalt roofs**, on olive ground.
- Violet is reserved for bloom. Pure red for wax. Bright cyan was tried for ley and rejected:
  the channels must sit *in* the street, not shout over it.
- Neutral noon is the primary lighting condition and the one every object is judged in.
  Night (lit windows, lamp pools, glowing ley) is a reward, not the identity.
- No bloom post-processing. Emissive only on lamps, lit windows and ley.

## 6. Vegetation and props

- **Candle-pine:** a bare trunk, then a tight column of drip-tiers with lighter rims, and a
  guttering tip bent into a hook. Generic low-poly cones were rejected at the first screenshot.
- **Inkwood:** pale trunk, broad blobby blue-black crown speckled with pale ink-berries.
- **Wax-thistle clumps:** small pale stalks with ochre buds; the ordinary weed of the verges.
- Street furniture: crook-pole lamps with a sip-pipe from the channel, hook-signs, zigzag
  fences, brass-hooped barrels, ink jars, bread crates, mile-seal posts, the waymark.
- Rule kept: if a prop could sit in a generic medieval village unchanged, give it a hook, a
  seal, or a zigzag until it can't.

## 7. Inhabitants

- **Apprentices** (hooded short cape, a red book under the arm), **journeymen** (hook-hat with
  a brass band, satchel), **masters** (tall ink hook-hat with a lantern on the hook, beard).
- Small, chunky, big-headed, ink-lined. They walk the streets between home and work, stand
  at wells and taverns, and go indoors at night. They never fight, cast, or fly.

## 8. Magic as a city system (as built)

- **Ley is a flow.** Springs and ley wells push pressure into adjacent channels; supply is
  allocated nearest-first, so the far end of town browns out when demand exceeds pressure.
- **Dead ends pool and bloom.** Bloom is fed only near a pooling tile; ward stones shrink it;
  scouring is free but temporary. Buildings beside bloom are warped: half output, unhappy
  residents.
- **Ley changes what buildings do.** Allotments beside a channel go strange and yield ink
  instead of bread (the most common surprise in playtests). Bakeries bake for the whole street
  only with ley. Lamps only stand on channels.
- **Charters** are earned by what the town is: population, ink made, things built. Each seals
  with a stamp ceremony and unlocks a small family.

## 9. Interface

- A **ledger**: paper panels, ink serif type, wax seals for charters and palette glyphs,
  a troubles list that names the spatial fix. The cursor ghost tells the truth: green or
  ink-red footprint, a ring for radius effects.
- Problems announce themselves in the world first (bloom, dark streets, people leaving) and in
  the ledger second.

## 10. Progression and pacing (measured with `tools/playtest.mjs`)

A day is 75 seconds. At 1× a competent build order seals Channels at ~3 min, Quills at ~7,
the Chancery at ~11, Wards at ~12, Masters at ~15 and the City Charter at ~28 (stone-gated).
Stone is the deliberate late bottleneck; bread is the deliberate mid-game one.
Failure is soft: bread runs short, streets go dark, bloom spreads, people leave. Recovery is
always spatial.

## 11. What Hookwell refuses to be

- Not a single hero tower with scenery around it.
- Not a floating island, not a crystal city, not a purple glowing anything.
- Not gabled, not thatched, not half-timbered-tudor-by-default.
- Not "pretty stylized low-poly" that lives on bloom and emissive windows.
- Not a spreadsheet: every system has a spatial consequence you can see.
- Not a combat game, not a hero game, not a spell-slinging game.

## 12. Medium and tools

Three.js, code-authored geometry through a small kit (`src/render/kit.js`: world-scaled UVs,
tented roofs, frustums, rim-tinted cones, merged meshes per material), procedural canvas
textures (`src/render/textures.js`), a depth/normal ink-line pass (`src/render/inkpass.js`),
and neutral daylight by default. Two project tools were built after real friction:
`tools/shoot.mjs` (the canonical inspection views, from the real camera) and
`tools/playtest.mjs` (a scripted player that runs the whole loop and prints the pace).
